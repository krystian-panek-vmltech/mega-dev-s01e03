The three problems

1 · parser: the shared parser reads dates month first (374 reports). Our tenants write the day first. The parser reads month first, so day and month swap: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.” It happens on every channel, for every day-first tenant. This is what #4471 was opened for, and the fix queued for it.

2 · hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type month first (33 reports). Under its date field booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY. People at day-first tenants do what the hint says, and the form then reads the date their tenant’s way, day first. The dates swap, so these look like the parser bug, but they come only through the form, only at booking-v3 tenants (alder.physio, pale.harbour, copper.studio). Fixing the parser does not change the hint. The fix is the form’s hint.

3 · one-day: the date moves by one day (5 reports). No swap of day and month moves a date by one day. “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May.” “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday.” “Every booking in June is one day off.” This is a time-zone or summer-time shift, not the parser. The desk had already marked these reports (desk-notes.json), but #4471 still counts them under parser.

Why one ticket hid three

  • Merged as one, read as one. Reports with many different subject lines went into #4471. Neither the subject (decision 0001) nor “it is about dates” tells the causes apart. Only the body and the tenant do.
  • Intake tags are a guess from the browser. An en-US browser got hint, the rest parser. The cause is the tenant and the way in, so the tag column above disagrees with the problem column in both directions.
  • The export repeats rows. Pages of the export overlap. Count each report id once.

What to do

  1. Keep #4471 for the parser. Close it when the parser fix ships and new parser reports stop.
  2. Fix the booking-v3 hint (issue #2 in the tracker). Do not wait for the parser fix.
  3. Hand the one-day move (issue #3) to whoever owns time zones in sched-prod. It needs its own reproduction: a meeting seen from two time zones, and a booking across the summer-time change.
  4. Split by what the body shows when a big ticket grows. Subject alone merges different bugs.
